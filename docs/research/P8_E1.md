## Reading (post hoc, not tested)

- Against the expectation recorded before training (D156): of 5,183 source 2 boxes only 1,102 were players not
  already boxed in agreement, "the rest are foot placement, which a detector does not fix", so the expectation was a
  small fall in misses and no position gain. The result went the other way on both.
- The labels can teach a miss. Source 1 keeps only players both our detector and YOLOX-X boxed in agreement; boxes
  only one model drew are dropped (D131), and source 2 recovers 1,102 of the players our detector missed
  (P8_teachers.md). A player the two models did not box in agreement, and that source 2 did not recover, stays
  unlabelled, so the new detector is trained to treat it as background. E1 also draws fewer non-ghost boxes than r23
  on every pool game (15,727 against 14,899 on gsw_bkn_2015, 9,643 against 9,251 on gsw_cha_2016, 16,825 against
  16,210 on gsw_sac_2015, from the score files in the P8 workspace). That fits fewer ghosts and more misses.
- The position and bias rows use only players both builds match. E1 matches 3 to 4 percent fewer player-frames than
  r23 (17,167 against 16,415, 10,961 against 10,568, 18,120 against 17,549 on the three pool games, same files), so
  part of the position gain may be players E1 lost rather than players it placed better. Better foot placement from
  source 2's boxes is one possible explanation, not a tested one. Neither offsets the misses under the registered
  rule.
- This is one seed. The per-game agreement makes it unlikely that seeds 1 and 2 would reverse the miss row, but that
  is a judgement, not a measurement, and it does not matter for the verdict (above).
- Any next arm (for example adding E-BARD's boxes, or keeping teacher boxes our detector misses outright) needs its
  own registration before training (D156).

## Caveats

- In sample for the court model: r23's court model was trained on the R1.3 games' labels, so position, bias and
  coverage are not held-out measures on the pool games (P8_baseline.md). Both builds use the same court model; only
  the detector differs. r23's player detector was trained on an outside Roboflow set (DEVLOG.md 2026-06-26), not on
  these games, so the detector comparison is not in sample for either side.
- The truth is the Mac camera truth (D99): it accepts fewer frames than Colab's R1.3 run and favours frames where
  the pipeline already works.
- The team row is the frozen best A/B mapping per window, an upper bound on a fixed mapping.
- gsw_ind_2016 stays apart (D98) and was not scored. No held-out game (sportvu/splits.json) and no D85 test game was
  read; the scorer's guard (tools.p8_common.guard) ran on every command.

## Post-mortem (measured)

Added 2026-11-24 after the D171 verdict. It tests the post hoc reading above ("the labels teach unlabelled players as
background") and gives the next arm (E1b) its numbers. It is a measurement only: nothing trains, no model is run, no
window is rebuilt, and the verdict above does not change. Tool: tools/p8_e1_postmortem.py; record:

### Definitions (fixed before computing)

Games: the 3 pool games (gsw_bkn_2015, gsw_cha_2016, gsw_sac_2015) only. tools.p8_common.guard and
tools.research_testset.refuse_cv_test run on the game list first. hou_cle_2016 has no corpus frames, so M1 does not
cover it; M2 on hou_cle_2016 is optional and, if computed, reported apart. No held-out game and no D85 test game.

Shared rules, all reused from the code that built the corpus or scored E1, none new:

- In-image SportVU player (M1): tools.p8_teachers.targets_in_frame, the rule tools.p8_e1data build used: the
  mirrored SportVU position projected through the inverse of the frame's camera truth lies inside the image (no
  margin).
- Box foot: the bottom centre of a box sent through the frame's camera truth (tools.p8_teachers.feet_ft).
- A box set finds a player when the one-to-one Hungarian match of its feet to the in-image players puts that player
  within 2.5 ft (CONFIRM_FT; tools.p8_teachers.found_targets, the misses rule).
- Our detector in M1 is the corpus's "ours" boxes: the production detector, the one the r23 builds run (player class,
  confidence 0.40). The teacher is YOLOX-X SportsMOT at 0.1471. Both are the raw detections stored in the corpus
  preds; nothing is run again.
- Camera third: tools.p8_scorecard.third_of on the SportVU y with sportvu.eval.near_side of the camera truth (near,
  middle, far), the thirds of the scorecard's near-field bias row.
- Expected box height: the pinhole camera of sportvu.camera.pose_from_h on the camera truth; the player's court point
  at height 0 and at a nominal 6.5 ft are projected with tools.p8_scorecard.project3d; the height is the vertical pixel
  distance. 6.5 ft is one fixed nominal height, not each player's own. Bins: under 50, 50 to 75, 75 to 100, 100 to 150,
  150 px and over; "no pose" when pose_from_h returns none.
- Edge: the projected foot lies within 40 px of the left, right or bottom image border, or within 40 px of the top.
  The margin (40 px, about half a typical player box height at 720p) is fixed here.

M1, label audit on the corpus frames (every frame of plan.json and manifest.json in the corpus):

- Labelled: the player is found (rule above) by the written label boxes, labels/<game>/<stem>.txt turned back from
  YOLO lines to pixels with the frame's image size.
- Label recall: labelled players over in-image players, per game and pooled.
- Every unlabelled player gets one box-source class: "r23 only" (our detector finds it, the teacher does not);
  "teacher only"; "both, IoU < 0.5" (both find it, and the box our detector's match assigns to it and the box the
  teacher's match assigns to it overlap at IoU under 0.5); "both, IoU >= 0.5" (both find it with agreeing boxes, so
  the label rule had them and still wrote no box within 2.5 ft; expected small); "neither". For "neither", a report
  only subcount: some box of either model has its foot within 5 ft (B4's final gate, sportvu.bench.MATCH_FT) of the
  player, nearest box, not one to one: a box near the player whose foot is placed badly, against no box at all.
- Unlabelled players are broken down by third, expected height bin, edge, and box-source class; every breakdown also
  shows all in-image players, so label recall per bin can be read.
- Report only: label boxes whose foot no in-image player claims within 2.5 ft under the same match.
- Labels that the classes imply E1b could add: the counts of "r23 only" and "both, IoU < 0.5" players (a box exists
  for them and SportVU confirms it).

M2, where E1's extra misses are (each pool game scored by the fold that held it out, as the registered pool):

- Builds: builds/r23 and builds/e1_<fold>_seed0 in the P8 workspace, on the windows the fold's score file
  (e1/<fold>__seed0__<fold>.json) scored. Each window is scored again by the pinned scorer's
  tools.p8_scorecard.score_window, truth camera, read only from the existing sidecars and cached references; a window
  whose cached references are missing is refused, not rebuilt. One game at a time under the heavy-job lock.
- Player-frame: one observation of score_window: an in-frame SportVU player (10 px margin) on a live wide processed
  frame. Missed: no box foot of the build (foot_stab through the camera truth) within 2.5 ft (MISS_FT), the scorer's
  missed-players rule. Check: the per-game totals of in-frame and missed must equal the score files' counts.
- Pairing by window, frame and player id: both found; E1 extra miss (r23 found, E1 missed); E1 extra find (E1 found,
  r23 missed); both missed. E1 extra misses minus E1 extra finds equals the change in the missed count.
- Both extra classes are broken down by third, expected height bin, edge, and corpus label status: the same window,
  frame and player in M1 (labelled, or unlabelled with its box-source class; "not a corpus frame" if absent). For the
  held-out game this status was not seen by that fold's model in training; it describes the player-frame as the
  label rule that built the training games' labels would have treated it.

What would support the post hoc reading, fixed here: (a) a material share of in-image players is unlabelled, and
many of those are players our detector boxes ("r23 only" or "both, IoU < 0.5"); and (b) E1's extra misses are
enriched in player-frames the label rule leaves unlabelled: the unlabelled share among E1's extra misses is larger
than the unlabelled share among all player-frames r23 found, on the pool and in the same direction on each of the 3
games, with a window bootstrap interval (2,000 resamples, seed 0, windows resampled whole) of the pooled difference
wholly above zero. If (b) fails, the reading does not hold as stated, whatever (a) shows.

M3, label oracle (added at the lead's request while M2 ran, written before any M3 number): on each pool game's
corpus frames, the written labels are scored as if they were a detector's boxes, beside our detector's raw boxes
(the corpus's "ours" player boxes at 0.40) on the same frames, with the scorer's own rules (sportvu/eval.py,
eval_frame, read from the code):

- In-frame player: a SportVU player whose projection through the inverse of the camera truth lies strictly inside the
  image with a 10 px margin (FRAME_MARGIN_PX). This is the scorer's in-frame rule, not M1's no-margin rule.
- Missed: an in-frame player with no box foot within 2.5 ft (MISS_FT), the nearest foot of all the frame's boxes, not
  one to one. Box foot: the bottom centre through the camera truth (the raw boxes have no stabilised foot; a build's
  sidecar uses foot_stab, so these rates are not the registered r23 numbers, which come from the tracked build).
- Ghost: a box whose foot lies more than 3.0 ft (GHOST_FT) from every one of the 10 SportVU players. Labels and our
  player boxes hold no referee class, so the ghost rate is ghosts over all boxes (a referee boxed counts as a ghost,
  as in the scorer's raw count).
- Boxes per in-frame player: all boxes over in-frame players. Duplicates: in-frame players with two or more box feet
  within 2.5 ft.
- Missed rate is missed over in-frame players; ghost rate is ghosts over boxes; pooled and per game. The teacher's
  boxes (0.1471) are scored the same way as a reference row.
- Interval: label minus ours for the missed and ghost rates, 2,000 bootstrap resamples, seed 0, corpus windows
  resampled whole.
- E1b ceiling, fixed here from the lead's note: a detector trained to reproduce these labels cannot be expected to do
  better than the labels. If the labels do not lower the missed rate below ours by about 0.02 (the size of E1's rise)
  without raising the ghost rate, the labels leave no headroom for E1b as built.

Checkpoint note (from the lead, confirmed in the installed package): Ultralytics 8.4.48 picks best.pt by mAP50-95
alone (ultralytics/utils/metrics.py, DetMetrics fitness weights [0, 0, 0, 1] over P, R, mAP50, mAP50-95), as "What
ran" now says (corrected in 0d3ff4fc; it first said 0.1 mAP50 plus 0.9 mAP50-95, the weighting of older releases).

### Reading (measured)

- Part (a): its first clause holds. One in four in-image players (13,803 of 55,147) is unlabelled at 2.5 ft. Its
  second clause does not: only 673 of them (4.9 percent of the unlabelled) are "r23 only" or "both, IoU < 0.5",
  players our detector boxes that the labels left out. Neither clause had a numeric threshold, so as registered the
  reading is supported only in part.
- Part (b), the registered test, holds. Unlabelled player-frames make up 19.5 percent of E1's extra misses against
  9.6 percent of everything r23 found. The direction holds on all 3 games, and the pooled window interval
  [0.0693, 0.1327] lies above zero. This is an association, not a mechanism. Difficulty explains it too: unlabelled
  player-frames are hard (r23 finds 3,870 of 13,593 of them, against 36,448 of 41,292 labelled ones), and a hard
  player is the one a changed detector is most likely to lose.
- Report only: the net rise on labelled player-frames (+1,592) is more than the whole net rise (+1,525); on
  unlabelled player-frames it is -67. E1 recovers 39.6 percent of r23's labelled misses and 9.4 percent of its
  unlabelled ones, which difficulty also fits. This measurement does not separate a training cause (initialisation,
  recipe, checkpoint choice) from a global label-noise effect: missing or misplaced labels could lower E1's
  confidence for all players, labelled ones included. M4 tests the threshold part.
- The labels score better than our detector's raw boxes on the same frames: 0.035 fewer misses and 0.043 fewer
  ghosts, both intervals wholly below zero on every game (gsw_cha_2016 smallest at -0.024 missed). Part of that gap
  is by construction. The 1,102 written source 2 labels were kept only where a teacher foot lies within 2.5 ft of a
  SportVU player our detector missed, the same SportVU truth M3 scores against. Those boxes cannot be ghosts and each
  removes a miss, so up to about 0.020 of the 0.035 missed gap (1,102 of 55,035 in-frame players) comes from the
  filter. A detector gets no SportVU filter at inference. The teacher alone, with no such filter, misses less than
  the labels (0.1966 against 0.2210) at a similar ghost rate (0.1397 against 0.1338).
- By the ceiling rule above, the labels leave headroom against r23's raw boxes, but less than 0.035 once the filter
  is taken out. E1's tracked rows suggest it did not reproduce its labels; its raw boxes were not measured here (M4).
  The caveat: these are raw boxes with bottom-centre feet, not a tracked build with stabilised feet. The registered
  r23 row (0.2654 on the scored frames) is not the raw 0.2556 here, so the gap is against raw boxes, not a forecast
  for a registered row.

Implications for E1b (proposals for its registration, not decisions):

1. Fine-tune from r23's weights instead of yolov8m.pt. E1 lost players that r23 finds and the labels box; starting
   from r23 aims to keep that recall. Whether the initialisation caused the loss is not tested here.
2. Before any new training, measure E1 seed 0's raw boxes on its held-out game's corpus frames over a confidence
   sweep, with the M3 rules. This needs a Mac CPU inference run and its own line. It separates a threshold loss
   (the production 0.40 was set for r23's score scale) from a learned one. If E1's raw boxes reach the labels'
   oracle rates at some threshold, the fix is the threshold, not the labels.
3. Masking or ignoring unlabelled players: low expected value as measured. Matched one to one, 5,170 in-image
   players (9.4 percent) have no label box of their own within 5 ft (report only). On those player-frames E1's net
   change against r23 was -50 (250 extra misses, 300 extra finds; report only). If E1b masks, it would mask only
   those; this measurement does not show that masking lowers misses.
4. Adding our detector's boxes: 673 labels (0.12 a frame), too few to matter. The 2,156 "teacher only" players are a
   merge artefact, not missing boxes. Keeping the teacher box where it reaches a player that the source 1 box misses
   would move the feet of up to 2,152 labels and add no box.
5. Checkpoint choice: best.pt is picked by mAP50-95 on pseudo-label validation, which rewards tight boxes, not
   SportVU recall. E1b could choose by the scorer's missed row on a training-game validation split.

Not measured: M2 on hou_cle_2016 (optional). It has no corpus frames, so the label-status split, which is the test
here, does not exist there. It was not run. E1's raw detections on corpus frames were not measured: that needs the
model run, which this post-mortem does not do (implication 2).
