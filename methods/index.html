How to read this page

Our numbers are copied from the reports in the code. Each one links to its file.

Published numbers are quoted from the notes we keep for each model. Those notes cite the paper and the table the number comes from.

Few papers measure a number the way we do. So each pair carries a verdict, quoted from those notes, on how far the two can be compared.

Each new test had its bar fixed before it ran. It runs first on the training games, then once on games set apart for it. It is adopted only if it clears the bar on both.

Some tests have no bar on the training games; they are scored once on the test games and say so.

The verdicts fall into three kinds

  • Roughly comparable
  • Not directly comparable
  • Nothing to compare

    Post hoc, not pre-registered: stepped down to one game, the target's reliability is about 0.04, which would meet H1's intent; part of the half-season reliability may be team offence shared by teammates (not measured).

Shot quality

Our notes on this model: docs/research/xfg.mdIts report: reports/xfg.md

Built on

  1. Chang, Maheswaran, Su, Kwok, Levy, Wexler, Squire. Quantifying Shot Quality in the NBA. MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference 2014 (research paper competition).
    What we take from it
    Shot quality as the make probability of a league-average shooter from SportVU features (location, defender distance, more), scored by MSE (Brier) under cross-validation. We use the same idea and the same feature family, but our model adds a shooter prior, so it is not a league-average-player quantity, and we select by log loss with folds grouped by game.
  2. Zadrozny, Elkan. Transforming classifier scores into accurate multiclass probability estimates. KDD 2002, pp. 694-699.
    What we take from it
    Isotonic regression (pool adjacent violators) as a calibrator for any ranking score; the paper notes PAV "overfits slightly the training data" but beats sigmoid fitting (Section 5 results). Ours fits it on inner out-of-fold scores grouped by game and adds an ad hoc clip to [0.02, 0.98].
  3. Vovk, Petej. Venn-Abers predictors. UAI 2014.
    What we take from it
    Venn predictors built on isotonic regression: each test object gets two probabilities (p0, p1), and under IID data one of them, selected by the true label, is perfectly calibrated (Theorem 1). Not used yet; it is the basis of xfg-E1.
  4. Vovk, Petej, Fedorova. Large-scale probabilistic predictors with and without guarantees of validity. NeurIPS 2015.
    What we take from it
    Inductive and cross Venn-Abers predictors (IVAP, CVAP) and the log-loss minimax merge p = GM(p1) / (GM(1 - p0) + GM(p1)) (Section 4). Section 5.2 shows plain isotonic regression can give infinite log loss when a test score falls outside the calibration scores; our clip is the kind of ad hoc fix the authors decline to use. Not used yet; xfg-E1.

From the notes

  • Brier skill is 1 minus Brier over the constant model's Brier, computed here from numbers read in each source.
  • The lane takes the shooter of an attempt from the play-by-play name matched to the tracking roster, and the names that fail are not random (two players with one short name, a later name, mojibake, a suffix). When the name does not resolve, the lane takes the player nearest the ball at release as the shooter (analytics/xfg/features.py, lines 30 to 31 and 273 to 275). In the training games 5477 out-of-fold attempts did not resolve by name: 5079 took the player nearest the ball, for the shooter prior and the contest features, and 398 had no shooter and so no contest features. Another 611 tracked attempts carry a name that fits two teammates and went to the first of them (reports/export/name_joins.json). The lane was not refit (PLAN.md D94, D95); the site withholds the shot-making values those joins touch.

Tests set up in advance

  • xfg-E1

    cross Venn-Abers calibration for a per-shot interval (the pilot)

    Fell short on the test games. Not adopted.

    Read it with its model

    Rank 8 in the list of every testExpected gain 3 of five, effort 2 of fiveTest data: Set A

    What it tests

    Replacing the frozen inner isotonic step of hgb_contest_prior by a cross Venn-Abers predictor (CVAP) on the same three inner folds keeps log loss (non-inferior) and gives every shot an interval (p0, p1); the log-minimax merge replaces the [0.02, 0.98] clip.

    The bar to adopt it, fixed in advance

    Adopt CVAP as the calibrator if the paired difference in log loss (CVAP merged minus frozen isotonic) has a 95 percent upper bound of at most +0.0005 on development games, and again on the D71 test set when scored once. +0.0005 is under 1.5 percent of the 0.037 contest gain. Separately, the site copy rule is fixed now: if the 95th percentile of p1 - p0 is under 0.01, the site states xFG as a point estimate whose calibration step moves it by under 0.01; otherwise each shot shows its (p0, p1).

  • xfg-E2

    second defender and contest geometry

    Set up, not run yet

    Rank 23 in the list of every testExpected gain 2 of five, effort 3 of fiveTest data: Set A

    What it tests

    Adding the second closest defender's distance, the angle between the shooter-to-basket line and the shooter-to-closest-defender line (contest from the front or from the side), and the closest defender's speed toward the shooter's line of shot, all at the release frame, lowers out-of-fold log loss.

    The bar to adopt it, fixed in advance

    Paired log loss difference (new minus baseline) at most -0.001 with its 95 percent interval excluding 0, on development games and again on the D71 test set scored once. Chang et al. found 0.0008 Brier between shot-and-defender distance and all variables, so a smaller change would not show in a coach's view.

Rebounds

Our notes on this model: docs/research/rebounds.mdIts report: reports/rebounds.md

Built on

  1. Maheswaran, Chang, Henehan, Danesis. Deconstructing the Rebound with Optical Tracking Data. MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference 2012.
    What we take from it
    SportVU positions of players and ball around a miss; offensive rebound rate depends non-linearly on shot location; which team rebounds becomes more predictable as the ball falls (Section 7). We predict the player, not the team, at one moment (the rim frame).
  2. Maheswaran, Chang, Su, Kwok, Levy, Wexler, Hollingsworth. The Three Dimensions of Rebounding. MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference 2014.
    What we take from it
    Rebounding as positioning at the shot, crash to the rim, then conversion; the value of a player's Voronoi cell, weighted by where similar misses land, is re-evaluated at the moment the ball is closest to the rim (Sections 3 and 4). Our rim frame matches their crash endpoint; we learn the probability from features instead of integrating a landing map over Voronoi cells.
  3. Guo, Pleiss, Sun, Weinberger. On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks. ICML 2017.
    What we take from it
    Temperature scaling, a one-parameter recalibration that keeps the ranking. Our tau is that idea applied to log q within each miss; top-1 is unchanged by it (reports/archive/rebounds.md).
  4. Cao, Qin, Liu, Tsai, Li. Learning to rank: from pairwise approach to listwise approach. ICML 2007, pp. 129-136.
    What we take from it
    A listwise loss built from probability models over the whole list of objects. Our model does not use it: it trains on single candidate rows. rebounds-E2 trains on the whole miss in this listwise way.
  5. Chen, Guestrin. XGBoost: A Scalable Tree Boosting System. KDD 2016.
    What we take from it
    Tree boosting on first and second order gradient statistics of the loss (Section 2.2), so any twice-differentiable loss, including a within-miss softmax, can be boosted. The tool for rebounds-E2.

  1. Wiseman, Shieber, Rush. Challenges in Data-to-Document Generation. EMNLP 2017.
    What we take from it
    RotoWire: NBA box scores to game summaries, scored by extractive metrics (an information extraction model run on the output). Their template system "scores very poorly on BLEU, but does quite well on the extractive metrics" (Section 6.1): relation generation precision 99.30 percent on test against 71.82 for the best neural row (Table 2). genai-E1 asks the same question of our checker. Ours checks every number by rule against the item's own facts, not by a learned extractor, and needs no reference text.
  2. Thomson, Reiter. A Gold Standard Methodology for Evaluating Accuracy in Data-To-Text Systems. INLG 2020.
    What we take from it
    Human accuracy annotation: three annotators per text mark each error as "Incorrect number", "Incorrect named entity", "Incorrect word", "Context error", "Not checkable" or "Other", merged by majority (Sections 3.2 to 3.5). Our human faithfulness protocol (genai-E2) uses their categories for what the checker cannot see; our readers are not paid crowd workers, and our texts are about 100 words, not 300.
  3. Hu et al. LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models. arXiv 2106.09685, 2021.
    What we take from it
    Freeze the weights and train low-rank matrices in each layer; "on-par or better than fine-tuning" on the models they test, with "no additional inference latency" (abstract). We use it because a 1.5B model cannot be fully fine-tuned in 16 GB.
  4. Kim, Rush. Sequence-Level Knowledge Distillation. EMNLP 2016.
    What we take from it
    Train the student on the teacher's output sequences; their student "runs 10 times faster than its state-of-the-art teacher with little loss in performance" (abstract, translation). Our A1 arm trains on the Haiku texts, which are also filtered by the checker, so it learns only from passing outputs.
  5. Yuan et al. Scaling Relationship on Learning Mathematical Reasoning with Large Language Models. arXiv 2308.01825, 2023.
    What we take from it
    Rejection sampling fine-tuning: k sampled paths per question, wrong ones filtered by answer and by a Python check, duplicates removed (Section 3); on GSM8K, LLaMA-7B rises from 35.9 (SFT) to 41.7 (RFT, k = 100), and "RFT brings more improvement for less performant LLMs" (abstract, Table 1), while for 33B "RFT does not improve performance compared to SFT". Our A2 is the same loop with our checker as the filter, k = 4.
  6. Qwen team. Qwen2.5 Technical Report. arXiv 2412.15115, 2024.
    What we take from it
    The 0.5B and 1.5B open-weight models are Apache 2.0, 32K context with 8K generation (Table 1); the 3B is under the "Qwen Research" license, so it is not used.

  • genai-E1

    a template writer against the Haiku texts

    Has run. Its result is in its write-up and is not shown on this page yet.

    Read it with its model

    Rank 2 in the list of every testExpected gain 3 of five, effort 1 of fiveTest data: genai test players and training-game possessions (reports/research/genai-splits.json)

    What it tests

    Can a writer with no model meet the checker as well as claude-haiku-5-5, at what cost in variety?

    The bar to adopt it, fixed in advance

    H1, the checker does not separate a template from the model: template minus Haiku pass rate has a 95 percent lower bound at or above -0.05. H2, the model writes with more variety: Haiku minus template frame variety has a 95 percent lower bound above 0. If H1 and H2 hold, the checker alone cannot show what a model adds; genai-E2 must report variety beside pass rate, and the value of a model is a question for the human protocol below. If H1 fails, the template is not a free substitute, and the failing rules say why. Grade, words, cost and time are descriptive and carry no threshold.

  • genai-E2

    a small open model, LoRA on the Mac, trained against the checker

    Set up, not run yet

    Rank 14 in the list of every testExpected gain 4 of five, effort 4 of fiveTest data: genai test players and training-game possessions

    What it tests

    Can a 0.5B or 1.5B open instruct model, fine-tuned on this Mac, write texts that pass the checker at a rate near the API model's, at no spend per text?

    The bar to adopt it, fixed in advance

    Paired bootstrap by player (scouting) or by game (possession), 2000 resamples, 95 percent percentile interval of the difference. - H1, training helps: the dev-best fine-tuned arm minus A0, per kind and size. Passes when the difference is at least +0.10 and its lower bound is above 0; fails when the upper bound is below +0.10; otherwise inconclusive. - H2, the checker as a teacher: A2 minus A1. Lower bound above 0: rejection sampling adds to distillation; upper bound below 0: it hurts; otherwise inconclusive. - H3, near the API model (scouting only): the dev-best arm with the repair loop minus Haiku as delivered. Lower bound at or above -0.05: non-inferior; upper bound below -0.05: inferior; otherwise inconclusive. At 81 players the interval half-width of a pass-rate difference is about 0.1 near rates of 0.8, so inconclusive is a likely reading and is stated as such. - H4, constrained decoding: A3 minus its arm, on the number_value failure rate and on the pass rate. Read as pass, fail or inconclusive on the pass rate by the H2 rule.
